Moving onto the [[Learning]] part, im just gonna copy paste what the article said coz it described it perfectly:

While this is an important note, the main success of MiSide’s horror comes from its mastery of tension and atmosphere. It utilizes a technique that I first learned from Doki Doki Literature Club!: terrify the player by putting something where it doesn’t belong. Take, for example, Mita’s house when we first enter. This house is _cozy_. The smooth textures, the dust particles in the air, the daylight gently shining through the gauzy curtains. The lighting rig in this house helps the environment to perfectly mimic an anime background; it brings to mind all of the amazing slice-of-life shows that this game’s target audience has seen. It is nostalgic, inviting, and safe.

Then, we go through Mita’s portal and accidentally blow a fuse. The house goes dark. And suddenly, it’s not the same house.
This is _not_ cozy. The windows are pitch-black. The dust particles are gone. There’s almost no light by which to admire the textures. The anime-style lighting rig as a whole is gone, replaced with a more realistic lighting rig coming from the cold overhead lamp. It casts shadows that don’t belong in this world. And it is that _not-belonging_ which makes it scary.

Remember that picture of Mita’s smile? The one that convinced me to play the game. It caught my interest because of how much it defied the cutesy (and dare I say _demure_) personality that she’d shown us thus far. Only small, polite smiles. This cheek-splitting grin morphs her face into a shape I didn’t know it could make; it _shouldn’t_ be possible for an anime girl to smile that big, but Mita does, and she hardly gives you enough time to process it. This not-belonging is applied expertly to the atmosphere in varying levels that make the game’s tension perfectly ebb and flow. _This_ is where MiSide’s attention to detail really shines; nothing is wasted when it comes to scaring the player shitless by relying on our expectations for a genre and subverting them.
